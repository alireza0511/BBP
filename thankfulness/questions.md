# Step 4: The questions (Thankfulness / شکر)

Stage in [brackets]. In the quiz, answer order is shuffled.

## 1. Noticing / دیدن نعمت

**Q1 (Ordinary situation).** At the end of an ordinary, uneventful day, a friend asks: “Anything good happen today?” What do you most likely say?

- [1] “Not really, nothing happened.”
- [2] I say “can't complain,” because that's what you say.
- [3] I mention a good thing or two, then add that I probably didn't deserve such an easy day.
- [4] I mention the one thing that sounds most impressive.
- [5] “Well, at least I'm doing better than my coworker.”
- [6] I think for a moment and name a few small good things — a good lunch, a nice call.
- [7] Several small things come to mind easily, including the people who made my day work.

**Q2 (Costly situation).** You've had the flu for a week. You missed work, you're exhausted, and you're behind on everything. Looking back at that week, what do you notice?

- [1] Mostly how much I lost and how far behind I am.
- [2] I tell myself to be grateful because others have it worse, but I don't really feel it.
- [4] I post something about being “grateful for the rest,” while mostly feeling miserable.
- [5] I'd feel grateful if it turned out to get me out of something I didn't want to do.
- [6] Once I feel better, I notice who checked on me and that my body recovered.
- [7] Even while sick, I notice small good things — a warm drink, a message from a friend.
- [8] Through the whole miserable week I still feel how much I was given — breath, care, a bed — without pretending it wasn't hard.

**Q3 (Someone who wronged you).** You mention that you enjoy your morning walk. A coworker who once took credit for your work laughs and says it's silly to be grateful for “little stuff like that.” What happens inside you?

- [0] I fire back by mocking the things they care about.
- [1] I stop paying attention to those things — maybe they're right.
- [3] I feel embarrassed for having said it and keep such things to myself from now on.
- [4] I start talking about my gratitude even more loudly, to prove a point.
- [6] I shrug it off and keep enjoying my walks.
- [7] I keep enjoying them, and I even notice the good things this coworker brings to the team.
- [9] I see my walk, my life, and even this difficult person as part of what I've been given — while keeping my distance from how they behave.

## 2. Thanking people / تشکر از دیگران

**Q1 (Ordinary situation).** A colleague stays late to help you fix a problem before a deadline. What do you do?

- [1] I'm so focused on the deadline that I don't really thank them.
- [2] I say “thanks” on my way out.
- [3] I apologize a lot for taking their time and insist on buying them lunch tomorrow.
- [4] I thank them in the team chat, where the boss can see it.
- [5] I thank them warmly — I'll probably need their help again.
- [6] I tell them right then, specifically, what their help saved me from.
- [7] I thank them then, and later, without being asked, I tell their manager what they did.

**Q2 (Costly situation).** You've spent all day moving house and you're exhausted. A neighbor you barely know carried boxes for an hour. It's late and you just want to collapse. What do you do?

- [1] I'm too tired to think about it, and I'll probably forget.
- [2] I wave and say thanks from the door.
- [3] I feel bad and insist on paying them, even though they keep refusing.
- [5] I thank them warmly — it's good to stay on a neighbor's good side.
- [6] I thank them sincerely before they leave.
- [7] I thank them, and a few days later I bring a small note or treat saying what their help meant.
- [8] Exhausted as I am, I go over, look them in the eye, thank them properly, and offer the same help whenever they need it.

**Q3 (Someone who wronged you).** Someone you had a bitter argument with last month quietly covered your shift when your child was sick. What do you do?

- [0] I tell myself they only did it to look good, and I say nothing.
- [1] It doesn't really register — we're not on speaking terms.
- [2] I send a short “thanks” text because I have to.
- [3] I feel like I owe them now, and I get awkward around them.
- [5] I thank them, mainly so I'm not in their debt at work.
- [8] Even though I'm still hurt by the argument, I go to them and thank them sincerely, without pretending the argument didn't happen.
- [9] I thank them sincerely and let this kindness genuinely change how I see them — while still talking honestly about what happened between us.

## 3. Using what you're given / به‌جا به‌کار بردن نعمت

**Q1 (Ordinary situation).** A friend gives you a beautiful, good-quality notebook they know you'll like. What happens to it?

- [1] It ends up in a drawer and I forget about it.
- [2] I use it when I'm going to see them, so they don't feel bad.
- [3] It's too nice to use — I keep it untouched so I don't ruin it.
- [4] I post a photo of it before I ever write in it.
- [5] I look after it because it was expensive.
- [6] I use it and take good care of it.
- [7] I use it fully, and later show my friend what I wrote or made in it.

**Q2 (Costly situation).** It's your one free evening after an exhausting week. An elderly neighbor knocks: they can't read an important letter written in a language you know well, and they must reply by tomorrow.

- [1] I pretend I'm not home.
- [2] I help because it would look bad not to, but I rush through it.
- [3] I help, then worry for days that I didn't translate it perfectly.
- [5] I help — they might be useful to me later.
- [6] I help them read and answer it, then rest.
- [7] I help, and tell them to come to me anytime they get a letter like this.
- [8] Tired as I am, I sit with them as long as it takes, glad this language I was given can be useful — and I still protect my rest afterward.

**Q3 (Someone who wronged you).** A former manager who treated you unfairly is also the person who trained you in the skill you now use most. A new colleague is struggling with exactly that skill.

- [0] I avoid using or passing on anything that person taught me.
- [1] I don't think about it — I just do my own job.
- [2] I help the new colleague only if I'm asked to.
- [4] I help them, especially when the team can see it.
- [6] I teach the new colleague what I know.
- [8] I teach them what I know, and can honestly say I learned it from that manager — without excusing how I was treated.
- [9] I pass on everything I know as a trust given to me, grateful for the gift even though the giver hurt me.

## 4. Not complaining or comparing / شکایت نکردن و مقایسه نکردن

**Q1 (Ordinary situation).** A friend tells you they just bought the kind of home you've been hoping for yourself.

- [0] I start pointing out everything that's wrong with their new place.
- [1] I congratulate them, feel a sting, and grumble about it later.
- [2] I say “congrats” because I should, and compare in my head all evening.
- [4] I congratulate them warmly in front of others, then complain about it privately.
- [5] I'm happy for them, as long as I'm still doing fine compared to them.
- [6] I'm genuinely glad for them.
- [7] I notice a twinge of comparison, let it go, and celebrate with them.

**Q2 (Costly situation).** This year you were passed over for a raise, while a coworker who does less got one. Money is tight at home.

- [0] I complain to everyone and try to turn the team against that coworker.
- [1] I grumble about it for weeks.
- [3] I tell no one, remind myself I should just be grateful to have a job, and quietly stew.
- [4] I act unbothered at work, but vent bitterly at home.
- [5] I'm okay with it as long as I'm still paid more than most of the team.
- [6] It stings for a while, then I remind myself what's good about my job.
- [8] I honestly don't envy my coworker, and I calmly ask my manager what I'd need to get a raise next time.

**Q3 (Someone who wronged you).** A former friend who spread rumors about you is now doing very well and posts about it often.

- [0] I hope it all falls apart for them, and I say so.
- [1] I keep checking their posts and feeling worse.
- [2] I mute them, but keep comparing quietly.
- [4] I post about my own successes, to show I'm doing fine too.
- [6] I don't wish them harm. I stop following them and get on with my life.
- [8] I can honestly wish them well, while staying clear of them.
- [9] Their success takes nothing from my life. I feel at peace with my portion, and with theirs.

## 5. Gratitude in hardship / شکر در سختی

**Q1 (Ordinary situation).** Your car breaks down on the way to work. You're late, and the repair will be expensive.

- [0] I take it out on whoever is around me.
- [1] My whole day is ruined.
- [2] I say “could be worse,” but I'm fuming inside.
- [3] I scold myself for being upset over something so small.
- [4] I joke about it at work like it's nothing, though it's eating at me.
- [6] Once the stress settles, I notice nobody got hurt and I can manage the repair.
- [7] Even waiting by the roadside, I notice good things — a stranger who stopped to help, that I'm safe.

**Q2 (Costly situation).** You lose your job unexpectedly, and the next few months are hard, both financially and emotionally.

- [1] I mostly see the loss; it takes over everything.
- [2] I keep saying “everything happens for a reason,” because people expect it.
- [3] I hide how scared I am, because I feel I shouldn't complain.
- [4] I make sure everyone sees how positive I'm staying.
- [5] I'll be grateful for it if I end up with a better job.
- [6] Over time, I see what stayed good: family, health, people who helped.
- [8] I grieve and worry honestly, and even in the worst weeks I hold on to what's still good.

**Q3 (Someone who wronged you).** Someone close to you betrays your trust, and the relationship ends painfully.

- [0] I make sure they pay for it.
- [1] All I can see is what they took from me.
- [3] I feel guilty for being so hurt, and keep it all inside.
- [4] I post about being “grateful for the lesson,” while inside I'm still burning.
- [6] After some time, I can see things from those years I'm still grateful for.
- [8] I name clearly what they did wrong and grieve fully, and I still don't let it erase the good years.
- [9] I protect myself and I grieve — and I find I can meet even this with the same trust and gratitude I have in good times.

## 6. Remembering past kindness / حق‌شناسی

**Q1 (Ordinary situation).** You hear that a teacher who helped you a lot years ago is retiring.

- [1] I barely remember them.
- [2] I'd sign a group card if someone passed one around.
- [3] I feel guilty I never thanked them, so I avoid reaching out.
- [4] I post a public tribute but don't contact them directly.
- [5] I'd reach out if they could still help me with something.
- [6] I remember what they did and think of them warmly.
- [7] I write to them myself and tell them what they did for me.

**Q2 (Costly situation).** An aunt who looked after you for a year when you were young is now elderly and would love regular visits. She lives an hour away, and your schedule is already full.

- [1] I've mostly forgotten that year — I'm busy.
- [2] I visit on holidays, because it's expected.
- [3] I visit out of guilt and feel heavy the whole time.
- [4] I visit mostly when the rest of the family will notice.
- [5] I visit when it's convenient and keeps the family happy with me.
- [6] I visit regularly, remembering what she did for me.
- [8] I rearrange my week to visit often, glad to give back some of what she gave me — without running myself into the ground.

**Q3 (Someone who wronged you).** A friend who stood by you through a hard time years ago later hurt you badly, and you're no longer close.

- [0] I tell people they never really helped me anyway.
- [1] I don't think about what they did for me anymore.
- [3] I feel I still owe them, which makes me avoid even thinking about them.
- [5] I bring up the good they did only when it helps the story I'm telling.
- [6] I can admit they helped me back then.
- [8] I honor what they did for me back then, even though they hurt me — without excusing the hurt.
- [9] Their kindness still lives in how I help others through hard times. I'm grateful for it, and I keep the boundary I need.


## 7. Nature and every creature / طبیعت و همهٔ جانداران

**Q1 (Ordinary situation).** You're filling a glass of water at the tap when a child asks you: “Where does water come from?”

- [1] “From the tap,” and I move on — I don't really think about it.
- [2] I give the textbook answer — rain, pipes — and leave it there.
- [3] I suddenly feel guilty about all the water I waste, and it turns into a lecture.
- [4] I give a beautiful answer about being grateful for water, mostly because other adults are listening.
- [5] “We're lucky — we pay for good water, not like some places.”
- [6] I explain its journey — clouds, rivers, the people who keep it clean — and say what a gift it is to have it.
- [7] We pause and thank the water together, and I tell the story of the clouds, the rivers and the people who bring it to us.

**Q2 (Costly situation).** Your old dog is sick. For weeks it has needed care in the middle of the night, the vet bills are high, and you're exhausted.

- [0] I lose patience with it and handle it roughly.
- [1] Mostly I feel how much it's costing me.
- [2] I do what has to be done, without much feeling.
- [3] I feel I owe it everything, so I skip my own sleep and meals for weeks to make up for it.
- [5] I'll keep caring for it as long as the costs stay reasonable for what it gives us.
- [6] I care for it patiently, remembering the years of company it gave us.
- [8] Even at 3 a.m., tired as I am, I sit with it gently, grateful for every year of its loyalty — and I ask family to share the nights so I don't break down.

**Q3 (Something that opposes, frightens or disappoints you).** You find a big spider in the corner of your bedroom. Honestly, you're afraid of spiders.

- [0] I kill it, then spray the whole house to get rid of every spider.
- [1] I get rid of it whatever way is quickest, without a second thought.
- [2] I put it outside because I've heard you're supposed to, but to me it's just a pest.
- [4] I carry it outside and film it, to show how kind I am to all creatures.
- [6] I carefully move it outside. It has its place in the world — just not my bed.
- [8] Even though I'm afraid, I catch it gently and release it, thankful for the insects it eats and its part in the world.
- [9] I see even this creature I fear as part of the same creation as me — its web, its work, its life — and I'm grateful it exists as I move it outside with care.

## 8. Thanking God / شکر خدا

**Q1 (Ordinary situation).** You wake up on an ordinary morning — healthy, with the day ahead of you. What happens in your first few minutes?

- [1] I reach for my phone; thanking God doesn't cross my mind.
- [2] I say the usual words of thanks or prayer out of habit, while thinking about other things.
- [3] I feel I'm never thankful enough, and start the day feeling guilty before God.
- [4] I talk about thanking God mostly when others can hear it.
- [5] I thank God mainly when I need something, or right after I get it.
- [6] I take a moment to thank God for the day, my health, and the people I love.
- [7] Before anything else I thank God, and small thanks keep returning through the day without any reminder.

**Q2 (Costly situation).** An injury keeps you in bed for a month. You're in pain, missing work, and the bills are piling up.

- [0] I turn bitter and mock the people around me who still pray.
- [1] I forget about God; all I can see is the pain.
- [2] I keep saying “thank God” because that's what people say, but I don't feel it.
- [3] I hide my pain, because complaining would feel ungrateful to God.
- [5] I promise God I'll be very thankful once I'm better.
- [6] I tell God honestly that it's hard, and still thank Him for what remains — family, a roof, the healing that's coming.
- [8] In the middle of the pain, I can cry honestly and still say alhamdulillah from the heart.

**Q3 (Something that opposes, frightens or disappoints you).** For a long time you prayed for something that mattered deeply — healing for someone you love, or work you badly needed — and it didn't happen.

- [0] I turn against God and try to pull others away from their faith too.
- [2] I keep up the rituals because it's expected, but inside I've closed the door.
- [3] I blame myself for not praying well enough, and carry that guilt.
- [5] I can be thankful again only once something else good comes along to make up for it.
- [6] After a while, I can thank God again for the many prayers that were answered.
- [8] I grieve, and even complain to God honestly, and still thank Him from the heart.
- [9] I trust that what was given and what was withheld both came from love, and I thank God in both — without pretending the loss doesn't hurt.
